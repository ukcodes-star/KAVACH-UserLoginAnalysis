Abstract
Listings for jobs, internships, hackathons and events can circulate after their application links
fail or their deadlines pass. A summary can also give a deadline the source never published.
Opportunity Engine collects student opportunities and checks them against their source pages.
Scouts collect listings from company career sites, social platforms and hackathon boards. A
verifier checks the application page before publication. The maintainer rechecks published links
and expires past-due listings, while an operator reviews cases that need judgement. The system
stores direct application or registration links. Workers coordinate through PostgreSQL, where a
trigger records changes to listing status and verification status. The report describes the public
API and web client and reports observations from the first two weeks of operation.

CHAPTER 1
Introduction
1.1 Problem statement
Students find jobs, internships, hackathons and fellowships on employer career pages, applicant
tracking systems, social platforms, aggregators and chat groups. A listing can stay in circulation
after the employer fills the role or closes applications. Even when the link works, a summary
may give a deadline or eligibility rule that differs from the original posting. Students have to
check several sources and can still miss openings.
Keeping a listing current takes more work than collecting it. Someone has to check whether
the application link works, confirm the deadline and eligibility on the source page, and repeat
those checks as the listing ages. Opportunity Engine makes those checks part of the job board’s
regular operation.
1.2 Motivation
I encountered these problems while applying to about fifty roles during my postgraduate studies
as I worked towards a full-time software engineering offer. Some aggregator listings were al-
ready closed on the employer’s site. Others described eligibility as 2026 batch or B.Tech only in
prose, so I could not filter for it reliably. Round-ups sometimes gave a deadline that the employer
had never published. I also found the same role listed under different titles or spellings of the
1

--- PAGE 17 ---
CHAPTER 1. INTRODUCTION
company name. Those experiences shaped what I wanted the board to record and check.
1.3 Objectives
1. Build a multi-vertical opportunity board covering jobs, internships, hackathons and events
for batches 2025–2030 and streams B.Tech, MCA and others, for India and remote roles
worldwide.
2. Capture listings continuously with autonomous agents (scouts) that read the source page
and record only explicitly stated facts.
3. Verify every listing before it is shown as trustworthy, and re-verify it on a schedule so that
dead links and expired postings leave the board automatically.
4. Enforce a strict link rule: the stored link must be the direct apply or registration URL, or
the row is skipped entirely.
5. Keep all verification decisions auditable, with a database-backed transition log and a hu-
man review queue for ambiguous cases.
6. Serve the board through a public API and a web client that any student can use without an
account, with an admin console for review and observability.
1.4 Scope and non-goals
The public board helps students discover and verify opportunities without an account. Students
complete their own applications on the source site. Opportunity Engine does not submit ap-
plications, send messages on a student’s behalf, or store student credentials or resumes. The
subscription form records an email address and filters. Scheduled delivery is planned, and the
site has no payment flow. The admin console requires authentication.
1.5 Organisation of the report
Chapter 2 places the project in the context of job boards and agent systems. Chapters 3 to 5
describe the architecture, workers and verification rules. The API, web client and database are
covered in Chapters 6 to 8. Chapter 9 describes deployment and operations. Chapter 10 covers
the deployment results and incidents.

CHAPTER 2
Background and Related Work
2.1 Job aggregators
General job boards such as LinkedIn, Indeed and Glassdoor collect postings from many sources.
A copy can remain searchable after the employer closes the original. Student-oriented boards
such as Internshala, Cutshort and Unstop can narrow the search, but eligibility often appears in
free text. Checking a source again costs an extra request and, in some cases, a browser session.
That cost grows with both the number of postings and the frequency of checks.
2.2 Applicant tracking systems
Employers use systems such as Lever, Greenhouse, Ashby, Workday, Phenom, iCIMS, Suc-
cessFactors, SmartRecruiters and Eightfold to publish jobs. An individual posting can expose
an application action or a closed notice that a search result may miss. A redirect or 404 calls for
a closer check of the URL and its history. The scouts use the posting page as their source for
these checks.

2.3 Multi-agent pipelines
The workers use a blackboard pattern. PostgreSQL holds the listing state, and each worker
reads rows at the stage it handles. Workers can restart independently because they exchange
records through the database. SELECT …FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED lets concurrent workers
claim different rows. The number of waiting rows helps operators locate a backlog.
Huyen covers application design and evaluation for foundation models [2]. Meta describes Muse
as a personal AI agent and explains the tool use and agent coordination supported by Muse
Spark [3, 5].
2.4 Trust and freshness in listing systems
The verifier holds a listing if it cannot establish that students can apply. A successful first check
can publish the listing with a pending verification status. The maintainer can change it to veri-
fied after a separate successful check. Suspected duplicates and other cases that need a decision
can enter the human review queue.
2.5 Gap addressed
Opportunity Engine collects listings from employer systems, social platforms and hackathon
boards in one place. It records eligibility that the source actually states and checks each appli-
cation link. The board exposes the resulting verification status. The database records changes
so an operator can trace how a listing reached its current state.

3.1 Overview
The web client reads listings from the API. The API and the scheduled agents use the same
PostgreSQL database. Workers pick up records in the state they handle and write back the result.
That arrangement lets workers restart independently and lets operators see a backlog through the
number of waiting records.

T wo fields track the listing independently. The status field records its lifecycle, including
found, verifying, published, expired and dropped. The verification_status field
records trust as pending, verified or flagged. A published listing can still be pending, and
a verified listing can later expire.
3.3 Deployment topology
The React frontend and Express API run on an Azure virtual machine. Nginx terminates HTTPS
and routes requests by hostname. PM2 serves the static Vite build and manages the Express
process. The API and agent workers use the shared Neon PostgreSQL database. The pub-
lic board is at https://opportunity-engine.interstellardevx.tech/ and the API is at
https://api.opportunity-engine.interstellardevx.tech/.
3.4 Technology choices
The agents, server and client use T ypeScript, with types defined for their respective interfaces.
PostgreSQL has the row locks needed to claim work atomically. Triggers record state changes,
and JSONB holds eligibility details that do not fit into fixed columns.
The Express server uses Prisma for database access and JWT authentication for protected admin
routes. The agents send raw SQL through the Neon HTTP API, so they need no persistent
database connection. React and Vite produce a static client. Hash routing keeps page routing
inside the browser.
3.5 Design principles
The first verification pass is intended to handle straightforward listings quickly. A later recheck
or a human review handles cases that need more evidence. A listing on hold stays out of the
verified set. When a worker’s report is unclear, operators can compare it with the database
records and findings files.

CHAPTER 4
Agent Layer
4.1 Organisation
The scouts, researcher, verifier, maintainer and watchdog run on schedules. Each worker has a
directory underagents/context/<agent>/. Its soul.md defines the worker’s role,rules.md
holds its instructions, memory.md holds brief working notes, and learning.md records lessons
from incidents.
The shared global/ directory contains two files. rules.md holds the common policy and
memory.md holds shared notes. Workers can propose additions to the notes. The main agent
decides which to accept and records accepted changes in Git.
4.2 Layer 1: Capture
The scouts collect listings on their own schedules. Before inserting a row as found, the shared
ingestion code checks field types, normalises the title and checks for duplicates.
The Fortune 500 scout checks one group of 25 companies each hour. A full rotation through
20 groups takes about 20 hours. A pointer file records the last completed group so the next run
can resume there. The portal scout also checks startup boards supplied by the researcher. It
prioritises YC startups and high-growth companies with public ATS boards.

4.3 Layer 2: Trust: the verifier
Every 15 minutes, the verifier claims a batch of found rows (Section 4.5). It checks that the
application link loads and offers an active way to apply. An inconclusive row stays on hold.
Possible duplicates or conflicts go to the review queue with the verifier’s reasoning. A row that
passes moves to published withverification_status still set to pending.
4.4 Layer 3: Freshness: the maintainer and the watchdog
The maintainer runs every 15 minutes. It expires past-due listings, rechecks links when due and
sends overdue verifications to the review queue. A clean recheck can award theverified badge
if the row is eligible for promotion under verification.promote_on_reverify. The policy
excludes flagged rows from automatic promotion.

CHAPTER 4. AGENT LAYER
The watchdog checks heartbeats every 15 minutes. It reports an agent as down once its last
heartbeat is more than twice the expected run interval old. The API assesses dashboard health
from the stored heartbeats and configured intervals. Operators should compare those intervals
with the scheduler when diagnosing a missed run.
4.5 Concurrency and claiming
Workers use an atomic common table expression built onSELECT …FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED
to claim rows and mark themverifying. Concurrent instances skip locked rows. If an instance
crashes, a claim older than fifteen minutes can be reclaimed.
Therun-wrap wrapper records the start and outcome of each run inagent_runs, including any
error details. The heartbeat table keeps the latest run per agent for the dashboard.
4.6 Layer 4: Judgement
Only an operator can merge rows, drop one based on judgement, or override an agent’s decision.
The admin review queue shows which field is in dispute, the suggested values and sources, and
the agent’s reasoning. The operator chooses a resolution and records it on the review.

CHAPTER 5
Rules and Policy Engine
5.1 Why rules live in a file, not in prompts
The workers load agents/context/global/rules.md at the start of each run. Its rules take
precedence over task instructions. That matters when a worker reads a page containing its own
instructions or receives a different task on a later run. Workers cannot edit the file. Changes are
proposed in chat, applied by the main agent, and committed to Git.
5.2 Hard boundaries
• Workers may find and verify opportunities. They may not fill forms, submit applications
or sign up for services.
• Workers may not read, write, log or transmit API keys, tokens, passwords or connection
strings. The Neon credential is in the Secure Vault. Worker configuration contains the
hostname and credential name.
• LinkedIn access is read-only. Workers may not send requests or messages, like content or
post. They stop and report any verification challenge.
• Workers leave the global rules and the user’s memory file alone. They write run notes to
a dated log.

5.3 The data contract
• Store the direct application or registration URL in link. Skip a candidate when a direct
URL or active application action cannot be confirmed. The place where it was found goes
in sourceUrl, such as a discussion thread, index or aggregator. Direct Google Forms
links (forms.gle/* or docs.google.com/forms/*) are recorded without opening the

• Use the hiring organisation for company. Normalise obvious spelling variants when writ-

5.4 The verification rules
• Confirm the deadline on the source page. A scout report or round-up can indicate that
applications may close soon.
• After a 404, check alternate slugs against the listing’s URL history before concluding that
the posting is gone.
• A result missing from a feed needs a separate check.
• Check quotations against the fetched posting. Some browser summaries have included
sentences that were absent from the page.
• Retry aBLOCKED page on the next rotation. A bot challenge or outage leaves the posting’s
status unresolved.
• Use a closed-application banner as evidence that the application has closed, even if a prior
check marked it live.

5.5. DEDUP AND ELIGIBILITY
5.5 Dedup and eligibility
The database has a unique constraint over the normalised (type, company, title, link)
tuple. A trigram match on normalised company and title (similarity ≥ 0.9) can send a possible
duplicate to review before publication.
The agents record explicit eligibility criteria. The student profile can move matching rows up
the ranking, while all listings remain available. The review queue handles ambiguous eligibil-
ity. Programme membership alone is not treated as a restriction. A training-first funnel with a
separate application site does not qualify as a direct application link.
5.6 The policies table
Administrators can edit the settings in policies through the authenticated API without rede-
ploying the agents. verification.promote_on_reverify controls automatic promotion af-

is overdue.

9.3. OBSERVABILITY
9.3 Observability
agent_runs records when each run starts and how it ends. The heartbeat table retains the
latest run per agent. The watchdog reads those heartbeats and reports how late an agent is. The
/admin/stats endpoint reports queue depths. A growing found queue, for example, gives an
operator a reason to check whether the verifier is keeping up.
9.4 Failure handling
A scout run can time out when its browser stops returning results. The run record then closes
with an error, and the last successful heartbeat is kept. The dashboard can mark the agent as
down once that heartbeat is old enough.
Early run tables omitted passes that found nothing. The watchdog once reported the events scout
as down for weeks even though it was running. Operators now check the run records before
acting on a down flag. The database or findings file is also used to confirm what a worker says
it collected.

10.3 Verification outcomes
Some application pages stopped working within minutes of a successful check. The pipeline
flagged dead links, and the audit trail helped find a defect in how the ingest path handled dupli-
cates. The promotion rules described in Chapter 4 exclude flagged rows from automatic promo-
tion. The public snapshot cannot show how often an incorrect promotion occurred over the life
of the system.
10.4 Discussion: what broke, and what it taught
The Reddit and events scouts hit repeated browser timeouts in November 2026. A stalled run
was closed as an error, and the dashboard kept the last good heartbeat. On another occasion, a
family of Workday tenants blocked the build machine’s IP . The simultaneous refusals pointed to
an access problem across those tenants.
The portal scout once returned sentences that the original posting did not contain. Workers now
compare quotations against the fetched text. A separate deduplication bug collapsed 55 distinct
postings because the key used type, company and title without the application link. The fix
normalises the fields first and includes the link in the key. The worker learning.md files record
these incidents and fixes.
